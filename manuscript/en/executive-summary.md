## Core Research Takeaways

### 01. Redefining the Concept: "Token Export" is a New Mode of Digital Service Export Coordinating Green Power, AI Compute, Foundation Models, and Cross-Border Networks

In the strict analytical sense adopted by this report, **narrow-sense Token Export** refers to economic activities in which large-model inference is physically executed on compute clusters located within China's customs territory (directly consuming domestic electricity and relying on domestic data center facilities) and delivered to overseas entities via cross-border APIs, hosted endpoints, or agent gateways to earn foreign exchange or cross-border service revenue<sup>[[1]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-1), [[2]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-2)</sup>. Offshore execution nodes under Chinese ownership or hybrid front-shop/back-factory architectures fall under broad-sense Token Export rather than being automatically counted as narrow-sense domestic compute exports; in national accounts and industry regulation, narrow-sense Token Export must be clearly distinguished and separately accounted for from pure overseas hardware/cloud leasing (offshore IaaS), pure open-weight model release without domestic inference execution, and general consumer SaaS application exports<sup>[[i]](../zh-CN/notes.md#note-i)</sup>.

### 02. Structural Demand Differentiation: Global Inference Workloads Diverge Sharply, with Asynchronous and High-Throughput Production Tasks Forming the Core Addressable Base

Cross-validation of platform telemetry from the *Anthropic Economic Index* against official enterprise surveys across 11 countries shows that within **Anthropic's first-party (1P) production API traffic sample**, **77%** of invocations exhibit automated machine-to-machine patterns (*Automation Patterns*), whereas within the **Claude.ai web conversation sample**, tasks associated with **Computer and Mathematical** occupational categories account for **36%** (these two metrics come from distinct service channels and sample denominators on the Anthropic platform rather than sharing a single denominator or representing a census of all global API platforms)<sup>[[11]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-11), [[12]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-12)</sup>. While real-time voice interaction and high-frequency algorithmic trading are constrained by transoceanic physical round-trip latency (`RTT > 150 ms`) and require deployment at local overseas edge nodes, workloads such as code generation, batch data processing, multilingual content production, and asynchronous multi-step agent reasoning tolerate Time-to-First-Token (`TTFT`) thresholds of **0.8 to 3.0 seconds or more**, constituting the genuine addressable market for transoceanic compute delivery.

### 03. Eastern 220 kV Grid Wheeling Charges are Actually Lower across China's 34 Provincial Grids; Western Tariff Advantages Depend Critically on Generation-Side Discounts and High Utilization

A complete-sample comparison of all **34 provincial and regional power grids** under NDRC Document *Fa Gai Jia Ge [2026] No. 1077* reveals a critical empirical fact: under two-part 220 kV industrial transmission and distribution (T&D) volumetric tariffs, high-load-density eastern grids such as Guangdong (**0.0449 RMB/kWh**), Fujian (**0.0462 RMB/kWh**), Jiangsu (**0.0518 RMB/kWh**), and Anhui (**0.0548 RMB/kWh**) actually charge lower volumetric wheeling rates than Gansu (**0.0693 RMB/kWh**), Qinghai (**0.0600 RMB/kWh**), and Northern Hebei (**0.0705 RMB/kWh**). Meanwhile, grid line-loss rates in Heilongjiang (**6.46%**), Eastern Inner Mongolia (**6.41%**), and Sichuan (**5.64%**) are substantially higher than in Shenzhen (**2.11%**), Gansu (**2.31%**), Ningxia (**2.58%**), and Western Inner Mongolia (**2.81%**)<sup>[[33]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-33)</sup>. Consequently, the **0.30–0.38 RMB/kWh** all-in delivered electricity cost advantage of western compute hubs stems primarily from low-cost wind and solar generation, low line losses in Ningxia/Gansu/Western Inner Mongolia, and the dilution of fixed capacity charges through **continuous cluster load factors of ≥75%**, rather than inherently cheap grid wheeling tariffs. If cluster utilization falls below **45%**, rising per-kWh capacity charge allocation rapidly erodes the east–west tariff spread<sup>[[37]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-37), [[38]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-38), [[39]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-39)</sup>.

### 04. Northwestern Hubs Exhibit Higher Location-Based Grid Carbon Factors across China's 30 Provinces; Physical Green Power Direct Connection and Market-Based GEC Retirement are Indispensable

Complete-sample data from the Ministry of Ecology and Environment (MEE) and National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) *2023 Power Carbon Dioxide Emission Factors* (published December 31, 2025, covering **30 provinces and 7 regional grids**) show that under **Location-based** grid-average accounting, only southwestern hydropower provinces—Yunnan (**0.1333**), Sichuan (**0.1564**), and Qinghai (**0.1796 kgCO₂/kWh**)—possess naturally low-carbon grids. By contrast, major northwestern and northern compute hubs such as Ningxia (**0.6187**), Inner Mongolia (**0.6479**), Hebei (**0.6516**), Shanxi (**0.6634**), and Xinjiang (**0.6021 kgCO₂/kWh**), due to coal-fired peaking power, exhibit grid-average carbon factors that exceed not only the national average (**0.5306 kgCO₂/kWh**, down from the 2022 historical baseline of 0.5366) but also southeastern nuclear- and hydro-rich provinces such as Guangdong (**0.4419**), Fujian (**0.4211**), Hubei (**0.4044**), and Zhejiang (**0.4974 kgCO₂/kWh**)<sup>[[118]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-118)</sup>. Therefore, northwestern and northern compute bases relying solely on standard grid power hold no carbon advantage in international compliance audits. They must transition to **Market-based** accounting via physical green power direct connection (*Fa Gai Neng Yuan [2025] No. 650* and *[2026] No. 688*) or vintage-matched exclusive retirement of Green Electricity Certificates (GECs, *Guo Neng Fa Xin Neng Gui [2024] No. 67* and *Guo Neng Fa Zi Zhi Gui [2025] No. 107*) to convert wind and solar electrons into internationally recognized low-carbon Tokens<sup>[[35]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-35), [[89]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-89), [[118]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-118), [[119]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-119)</sup>.

### 05. 30-Scenario Cross-Border Techno-Economic Analysis (TEA) and Global Sensitivity: Effective Utilization Main Effect Explains 71.17% of Cost Variance (Total-Order Index 0.7336), with 42.6%–65.3% Serving as the Transoceanic Break-Even Threshold

Our 30-scenario Techno-Economic Analysis (`M02`: 4 Chinese supply nodes × 3 overseas target markets × 2 standard workload classes + 6 overseas local benchmarks) and global variance sensitivity numerical estimation (`M03`) demonstrate that **effective business utilization** (first-order main effect `S_i = 0.7117`, total-order index including interactions `S_Ti = 0.7336`) and **annualized hardware & facility capital cost** (`S_i = 0.1372`, `S_Ti = 0.1506`) have first-order main effects that additively explain **84.89%** of unit Token delivery cost variance (the arithmetic sum of their total-order indices `S_Ti` is `0.8842`, which includes overlapping interaction terms and does not represent non-overlapping joint variance share). When serving Southeast Asian and Middle Eastern markets, northwestern wind-solar-storage hubs and southwestern hydropower hubs require break-even utilization rates (`u*`) of only **42.6% to 44.6%** (`M03B`) to achieve full-cost parity against local nodes in Singapore (**$0.753/1M Tokens**) and the Middle East. Under the `M06` reference baseline cost (**$0.7064/1M Tokens**, with a 1,000-node cluster producing **82.8 trillion** qualified Tokens annually at 52% baseline utilization), raising effective utilization by 10 percentage points (from 52% to 62%) via cross-time-zone scheduling directly reduces unit cost by **$0.0996/1M Tokens** and saves **$8.25 million/year** per 1,000-node cluster on a standalone basis (evaluated individually from the same reference baseline via single-factor finite differences, a 15% hardware CapEx reduction and a 20 percentage-point increase in KV cache hit rate yield standalone annual savings potentials of **$7.43 million/year** and **$5.23 million/year**, respectively; their arithmetic sum of **$20.91 million/year** reflects the sum of individual single-factor potentials rather than a simultaneous joint-intervention outcome due to nonlinear interaction)<sup>[[33]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-33), [[61]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-61), [[102]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-102)</sup>.

### 06. Four-Factor LMDI Electricity Decomposition and Domestic Value-Added (DVA) Retention: Algorithmic and Hardware Efficiency Gains Offset 250.3 TWh, while Indigenous Compute Synergy Lifts DVA to 88.5%

A complete, residual-free Logarithmic Mean Divisia Index (`LMDI`, `M04` & `M02B`) decomposition of China's data center electricity growth from 2024 to 2030 (rising from **102 TWh** to **277 TWh**, a net increase of **+175.0 TWh**) shows that inference volume expansion (**+335.8 TWh**) and reasoning complexity deepening via long Chain-of-Thought (**+107.8 TWh**) are the primary upward drivers, whereas hardware-software energy efficiency gains such as Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and low-bit quantization (**-250.3 TWh**) alongside liquid-cooling PUE retrofits (**-18.3 TWh**) jointly offset **268.6 TWh** of potential electricity demand. However, when falling unit inference prices stimulate elastic demand expansion (price elasticity > 1, Jevons rebound effect), efficiency improvements still coincide with net growth in total electricity consumption. Meanwhile, our stage-wise cost-structure value-added allocation model referencing OECD Trade in Value Added (`TiVA`) concepts (`M05`) indicates that progressing across the four formal modes in `M05`—from **"Mode A: Imported High-End Compute Hardware Pure Bare-Metal IaaS Leasing" (31.5% DVA)**, through **"Mode B: Imported/Hybrid Compute Cluster + Cross-Border Standard Token API Direct Connection" (58.0%)** and **"Mode C: Overseas Edge Compliant Front-Shop + Western Green Compute Back-Factory + Vertical Agent Delivery" (69.0%)**, to **"Mode D: Full Indigenous Inference NPU Cluster + Western/Southwestern Clean Power + Domestic Open-Weight Foundation Model" (88.5%)** (distinct from the legacy auxiliary baseline Mode A0 "Pure Overseas Cloud Rental & API Resale" at ~16% domestic retention)—raises the **Domestic Value-Added (`DVA`) retention ratio** per $100 of exports from **31.5%** to **88.5%**<sup>[[114]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-114), [[116]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-116)</sup>.

## ES.1 Core Proposition: Why is the Global Division of Compute Labor Undergoing Structural Reconstruction in the Era of Scaled Inference?

Over the past three years, global artificial intelligence infrastructure investment was anchored in pre-training **Scaling Laws**. Training a frontier foundation model with trillions of parameters required concentrating tens of thousands of homogeneous high-end accelerators within a single hyperscale campus interconnected by hundreds of Tbps of lossless high-speed fabrics. During that phase, the primary binding constraints on data center siting were **single-site grid interconnection capacity and physical chip density**; once pre-training completed, model weights could be distributed as static files, making production fundamentally a one-off, capital-intensive R&D activity.

Entering 2025–2026, however, the widespread adoption of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, test-time compute scaling, and enterprise multi-step autonomous agents has fundamentally shifted the center of gravity of global compute consumption. Every user query, automated code completion, and cross-border financial document audit no longer triggers a millisecond database lookup, but rather a continuous generation stream of thousands to tens of thousands of Tokens. **Large-model inference has thus evolved from intermittent software invocation into a 24/7 continuous "digital industrial assembly line" heavily dependent on sustained electrical power and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) throughput.**

This industrial transformation exposes a stark spatial mismatch between global compute supply and demand:
- **On the demand side**, high-paying enterprise AI markets are concentrated in North America, Western Europe, Japan, South Korea, and Singapore, where core metropolitan hubs face aging power grids, substation interconnection queues stretching **4 to 7 years**, elevated industrial electricity tariffs, and tightening carbon budgets.
- **On the supply side**, building on more than a decade of utility-scale renewable energy deployment and the eight national compute hubs under the **"East-Data-West-Compute" (*Dongshu Xisuan*)** initiative, China has accumulated substantial wind, solar, and hydro power capacity alongside standardized rack infrastructure across western provinces such as Inner Mongolia, Ningxia, Gansu, Guizhou, and Yunnan. Combined with high-efficiency model architecture innovations (exemplified by DeepSeek and Qwen), Chinese providers have reduced published API pricing per million Tokens to **1/5 to 1/20** of comparable international proprietary models<sup>[[45]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-45), [[54]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-54)</sup>.

While "Compute Export," "Token Export," and "Compute-Electricity Synergy" have attracted intense interest across government, industry, and academia, existing analyses remain largely siloed within single disciplines. Energy and infrastructure studies focus on western renewable tariffs and rack counts while rarely tracing transoceanic submarine cable latency penalties, international gateway routing, or data sovereignty barriers in European and U.S. target markets. Conversely, technology and financial analyses focus on model benchmarks and published API list prices, frequently conflating offshore cloud rental revenues or general SaaS subscriptions with domestic compute exports, without integrating generation-side and grid wheeling tariffs, AIDC facility depreciation, submarine/terrestrial fiber transport, dual-jurisdiction compliance, and per-million-Token financial settlement into a verifiable cost chain.

To bridge this gap, this study constructs an end-to-end analytical framework tracing the physical conversion, institutional boundaries, and commercial economics from **a single kilowatt-hour of western green electricity to one million delivered Tokens at an overseas terminal**. To quantify the net addressable share of global nominal AI spillover demand after passing through physical, regulatory, sovereignty, and profitability filters, **Exhibit ES.1** illustrates the five-stage screening funnel from "① Global Broad AI Spillover Base" to "⑤ All-In Energy-Carbon & Margin Filter."

## ES.2 Clarifying Industrial Boundaries: The Five-Stage Physical Conversion Chain and Four Critical Structural Bottlenecks

In current industry and policy discussions, "Token Export," "Compute Export," and "AI Application Export" are frequently conflated. To ensure rigorous market sizing and policy design, Chapter 1 of this report establishes a unified **"Five-Stage Physical Conversion and Institutional Accounting Framework"** that runs throughout the entire volume:

- **Stage 1 (Energy Substrate — Watts):** Wind, solar, hydro, thermal, and nuclear power from generation plants traverses provincial high-voltage transmission grids, transformer losses, energy storage balancing, and ancillary service cost pools to reach the data center fence line, forming the **All-in Delivered Tariff (LCOE to Rack)**.
- **Stage 2 (Facility Base — Racks & Raw Compute):** Electrical power passes through uninterruptible power supplies (UPS/HVDC) and liquid/air cooling systems (governed by facility **PUE**) to drive GPU/NPU accelerator arrays and High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM), converting kilowatts into effective floating-point and tensor throughput after deducting cluster interconnect overhead and idle power draw.
- **Stage 3 (Algorithmic Engine — Token Generation):** Scheduled by inference engines (`vLLM`, `SGLang`, `TensorRT-LLM`, `MindIE`) and optimized attention kernels (such as `FlashMLA`), hardware throughput is transformed into two distinct physical phases: compute-bound input **Prefill** and memory-bandwidth-bound output **Decode** Token streams.
- **Stage 4 (Cross-Border Channel — Transoceanic Bits):** Generated Token sequences travel across domestic backbone networks, international communication business gateways, cross-border terrestrial cables or submarine optical cables, overseas Internet Exchange Points (`IXPs`), and edge Points of Presence (`PoPs/CDNs`), spanning thousands of kilometers and TLS/HTTP/QUIC protocol stacks to reach overseas client terminals.
- **Stage 5 (Commercial & Statistical Loop — Foreign Exchange & Value Added):** Overseas developers or enterprise customers settle payments in USD, EUR, or other currencies based on per-million-Token usage, Provisioned Throughput, or seat subscriptions. Flowing through cross-border payment gateways, offshore contracting entities, and transfer-pricing frameworks, these revenues ultimately register as **Computer Services (`SI2`) exports** in China's Balance of Payments (`BOP`) and domestic compute asset returns<sup>[[1]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-1), [[2]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-2)</sup>.

By tracing this five-stage chain and conducting complete-sample empirical verification across all **34 provincial power grids**, **30 provincial carbon emission factors**, **30 WTO reference markets**, and **17 national compute interconnection regional nodes**, this report finds that scaling China's green compute global delivery from early developer adoption into a multi-billion-dollar mainstream infrastructure trade hinges on resolving **four structural bottlenecks**:

1. **Statistical Classification and Balance-of-Payments Attribution (Chapter 1 Core Finding):** While the United Nations *2025 System of National Accounts* (`SNA 2025`) and the IMF *Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Seventh Edition* (`BPM7`) have incorporated artificial intelligence systems and digital intermediation platforms into macroeconomic accounting frameworks<sup>[[1]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-1), [[2]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-2)</sup>, national customs and foreign exchange statistics under "Computer Services (`SI2`)" or "Digitally Deliverable Services (`DDS`)" do not yet separately itemize Token-level cross-border API calls<sup>[[3]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-3), [[4]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-4)</sup>. Moreover, when Chinese-developed models are invoked globally via overseas cloud hosts (e.g., AWS Bedrock, Azure AI) or third-party aggregation routers (e.g., OpenRouter) running on North American or European servers, the resulting revenues are recorded primarily as offshore entity output or minor intellectual property royalties, which must be separated from domestic cluster direct supply when measuring China's indigenous compute export performance<sup>[[i]](../zh-CN/notes.md#note-i), [[ii]](../zh-CN/notes.md#note-ii)</sup>.
2. **Global Demand Structure and Physical Latency Screening (Chapter 2 Core Finding):** Cross-calibrating complete-sample official enterprise surveys across 11 jurisdictions (U.S. Census Bureau BTOS, Eurostat, Singapore IMDA, South Korea NIA, Japan MIC, UK ONS, Statistics Canada, Brazil Cetic.br, India MoSPI, Saudi GASTAT, and Malaysia DOSM) against WTO `SI2` import series across 30 reference markets reveals a substantial gap between headline AI adoption and actual paid deployment (for instance, **32.9%** of South Korean enterprises report using AI, whereas only **8.3%** report actual AI investment or expenditure)<sup>[[13]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-13), [[15]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-15), [[16]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-16)</sup>. Simultaneously, the UK Competition and Markets Authority (`CMA`) cloud services final decision confirms that data egress fees and committed-spend discounts create strong incumbent cloud lock-in<sup>[[24]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-24), [[25]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-25)</sup>. Latency-tolerant (`TTFT > 1 s`), cost-sensitive, and PII-desensitized batch and asynchronous production workloads represent the primary customer segment capable of overcoming cloud switching barriers to utilize Chinese green compute hubs.
3. **All-In Tariffs across 34 Domestic Grids, Carbon-Factor Reversal across 30 Provinces, and Cross-Grid Compute Scheduling (Chapters 3 & 11 Complete-Sample Corrections):** Complete-sample empirical data overturn two widespread industry misconceptions:
   - *First, the assumption that "western grid wheeling charges are naturally lower than eastern grids" is false.* Across all 34 provincial and regional grids under *Fa Gai Jia Ge [2026] No. 1077*, 220 kV two-part volumetric transmission tariffs in Guangdong (0.0449 RMB/kWh), Fujian (0.0462 RMB/kWh), and Jiangsu (0.0518 RMB/kWh) are lower than in Gansu (0.0693 RMB/kWh) and Qinghai (0.0600 RMB/kWh), while line losses in Eastern Inner Mongolia (6.41%), Heilongjiang (6.46%), and Sichuan (5.64%) are relatively high. The true tariff competitiveness of western hubs derives from low generation-side wind/solar prices, low line losses in Ningxia (2.58%) and Gansu (2.31%), and **the dilution of fixed capacity charges via ≥75% continuous load factors**<sup>[[33]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-33), [[37]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-37), [[38]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-38)</sup>.
   - *Second, the assumption that "western compute is naturally low-carbon under location-based accounting" is false.* Across all 30 provinces in MEE's *2023 Power Carbon Dioxide Emission Factors* (national average **0.5306 kgCO₂/kWh**), aside from hydro-rich Yunnan (0.1333), Sichuan (0.1564), and Qinghai (0.1796 kgCO₂/kWh), location-based grid factors in Ningxia (0.6187), Inner Mongolia (0.6479), Xinjiang (0.6021), and Shaanxi (0.6335) all exceed those of Guangdong (0.4419), Fujian (0.4211), and Zhejiang (0.4974)<sup>[[118]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-118)</sup>. Consequently, northwestern compute hubs must pair physical green power direct connection (*Fa Gai Neng Yuan [2025] No. 650* / *[2026] No. 688* and proximate-consumption pricing under *Fa Gai Jia Ge [2025] No. 1192*) or vintage-matched GEC retirement (*Guo Neng Fa Zi Zhi Gui [2025] No. 107*) to switch to **Market-based accounting** (applying lifecycle factors of **0.0324 kgCO₂e/kWh** for wind and **0.0520 kgCO₂e/kWh** for solar), while leveraging MIIT's 17 regional compute interconnection nodes to raise cross-network schedulable compute above the current **10.8%** baseline<sup>[[35]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-35), [[40]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-40), [[118]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-118), [[119]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-119), [[123]](../zh-CN/references.md#ref-123)</sup>.
4. **Commercial Compliance, Submarine Cable Routing, and Enterprise Service Delivery (Chapters 5–10 Core Finding):** Leveraging the 2025–2026 commissioning wave of four major Asia-Pacific submarine cables (`SJC2`, `SEA-H2X`, `ADC`, `ALC`) and four newly approved international communication business gateways (**Nanning, Qingdao, Kunming, and Haikou**), alongside Free Trade Zone "inbound data processing (*Laishu Jiagong*)" regimes in Shanghai, Hainan, and Shantou, Chinese compute and model providers must deliver enterprise-grade services backed by **99.95% SLA availability**, independent **SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001** audits, and verifiable **Zero Data Retention (`ZDR`)** guarantees to move up the global value chain.

To move beyond static policy summaries or descriptive data tables, this report integrates an end-to-end quantitative modeling pipeline linking **empirical model outputs, structural insights, concrete application scenarios, and targeted operational actions**. Across the volume, we deploy eight quantitative methods: Kaya-LMDI residual-free decomposition (`M04`, `M02B`), two-part tariff nonlinear inversion and bottom-up partial identification (`M01`, `M01B`), semi-log hedonic GPU price regression (`M06C`), complex network betweenness centrality (`M07B`), Brent's root-finding algorithm for 3D break-even thresholds (`M03B`), convex optimization with Lagrangian dual shadow prices (`M04B`), Sobol global variance sensitivity and single-factor finite-difference cost analysis (`M03`, `M05B`, `M06`), and regional Theil entropy decomposition (`M06B`). Full methodological formulas, parameter provenance, and scope boundaries are documented in [`docs/en/methods-and-boundaries.md`](../../docs/en/methods-and-boundaries.md).

## ES.3 Overview of the Six-Part, Twelve-Chapter Research Architecture

To systematically address these engineering, economic, and institutional questions, the report is organized into **6 Parts, 12 Chapters, and 54 Sections**, connecting macroeconomic trade accounting, global demand screening, domestic green power and compute supply, global hyperscaler competition, submarine and terrestrial fiber topology, cross-border service engineering, dual-jurisdiction regulatory compliance, unit economics and regional division of labor, and phased policy/enterprise action roadmaps. **Exhibit ES.3** summarizes the complete 12-chapter architecture, core research propositions, authoritative data baselines, and key analytical deliverables.

Every part addresses a distinct domain constraint while sharing harmonized quantitative parameters—all-in delivered electricity tariffs, transoceanic fiber RTT latency, CAC Order No. 16 thresholds, and Levelized Cost of Token (`LCoT`)—providing a rigorous, reproducible decision framework for policymakers, regional compute hubs, and global AI service providers.
